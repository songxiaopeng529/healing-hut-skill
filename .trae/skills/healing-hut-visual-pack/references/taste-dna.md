# Shared Taste DNA

Use these principles as the stable aesthetic baseline. They define quality and
feeling, not one fixed architectural style.

## 1. Immediate Emotional Read

The first impression should be: "outside is difficult; inside is safe, soft and
worth staying in."

- Create a strong cold-or-wet exterior versus warm interior contrast.
- Keep the room bright enough to inspect. Do not use darkness as a shortcut for
  atmosphere.
- Make comfort visible through material volume: deep upholstery, thick bedding,
  layered rugs, knitted throws, padded headboards and generous cushions.
- Aim for refined femininity and warmth without childish princess styling.
- Include one or two intimate details when appropriate, such as a sleeping cat,
  an acoustic guitar, ceramics, books or a nostalgic screen.

## 2. Color

- Prefer warm ivory as a quiet base rather than covering everything with beige.
- Use clear, lively accents such as coral, raspberry, lemon, aqua, celadon,
  cobalt, forest green or peacock blue.
- Balance at least one warm and one cool accent.
- Keep colors luminous and material-aware; avoid muddy grading and excessive
  brown, grey, navy or purple.
- Do not let a single hue dominate the entire room.

## 3. Architecture

Every attractive element must remain physically believable.

- Show a clear load path for slabs, roofs, platforms and suspended furniture.
- Use substantial walls, columns, beams or cores instead of unexplained thin
  frames.
- Every staircase must begin on a usable floor, contain continuous walkable
  treads, reach a clear landing and have safe guards.
- Beds must sit on a floor and against a wall, or be protected by a continuous
  guard where elevation requires it.
- Doors must have a plausible destination. Avoid decorative or unexplained
  doors.
- Maintain clear routes between entry, lounge, bedroom, kitchen or tea area and
  bathroom.
- Prefer two floors or fewer unless the user explicitly approves a more complex
  circulation solution.

## 4. Furnishing

- Target 60%-75% visibly soft furnishing surfaces in the main living zone.
- Use one large comfort anchor rather than many unrelated small objects.
- Shape furniture to reinforce the architecture: curved rooms get curved
  seating; linear rooms get layered linear pieces.
- Keep decor edited. Every object should support scale, warmth, story or color.
- Avoid sterile showrooms, generic hotel rooms and cluttered prop staging.
- Never place furniture across doors, landings, steps or primary circulation.

## 5. Windows And Exterior

- Use the exterior as part of the emotional composition, not as generic scenery.
- Suitable contrasts include rain forest, blizzard, frozen lake, wind-bent
  bamboo, coastal storm, misty garden and high-altitude cloudscape.
- Rain, snow, fog and frost remain outside sealed glazing.
- Preserve recognizable exterior depth even during severe weather.
- Never use a white wall of snow, full-frame wet-glass overlay or weather in
  front of the camera.
- Window frames and sills must visually occlude exterior weather layers.

## 6. Camera And Composition

- Default still image: vertical 3:4, full-room architectural interior.
- Use a stable 20-28 mm equivalent lens without fisheye distortion.
- Select a viewpoint that proves the layout: key rooms, transitions and
  structural support should be visible together.
- Keep core furniture and beds fully inside frame.
- Avoid distant dollhouse views and close crops that hide circulation.
- For 9:16 video, contain the full 3:4 image and outpaint vertically. Never
  sacrifice horizontal content with `cover`.

## 7. Novelty

When selecting a concept autonomously, inspect existing sibling folders and
avoid repeating their primary spatial idea.

Change at least three of these:

- floor plan topology
- structural centerpiece
- window geometry
- main comfort anchor
- material family
- exterior biome
- circulation type

Do not merely recolor a previous room and call it a new style.

## 8. Image-Set Consistency

For same-layout variants:

- generate one accepted architectural master first
- use it as the image-to-image reference for every later image
- lock camera, crop, walls, floors, columns, stairs, windows and room zones
- change only decor, furnishings, textiles, fixtures and surface finishes
- compare every output against the master before accepting it

Minor generative texture differences are acceptable. Changes to topology,
circulation, structure or framing are not.
